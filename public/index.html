Passkey demo

Sign up and sign in with your fingerprint, face, phone or a security key. No password exists anywhere in this app.

1. Create an account

Your device makes a key pair. The private key stays with you, and only the public key goes to the server.

2. Sign in

No username to type. The server sends a one-time challenge and your device signs it.

3. Try to cheat

Pretend an attacker captured your last sign-in and sends it again. It should be refused, because every sign-in signs a new challenge.

What the server stores

Everything it holds about you. If this leaked, there would be no password to crack or reuse.

    This demo

    You play the user, and the page shows you what the website's server does behind the scenes.

    Create an account
    Your device makes two matching keys. The private key stays on your device, and the public key goes to the server. It's like giving a website a lock that only your key can open.
    Sign in
    The server sends a random one-time code, called a challenge. After your fingerprint, face or PIN, your device signs it with the private key. The server checks the signature with the public key, so the private key is never sent.
    Try to cheat
    This sends your old signature again, as an attacker might. Each sign-in has a new challenge, so the old signature doesn't match and the server refuses it.
    What the server stores
    Your username, the public key and a few details about the passkey. A public key can't be used to sign in, so there is no password to steal.

    The demo keeps accounts in memory, so it forgets them when the server restarts. It shows passkeys only, and it doesn't use a digital wallet.

    FIDO

    FIDO stands for Fast IDentity Online. The FIDO Alliance is a group of companies and organisations that writes shared rules for signing in without passwords. The rules are open and free to use.

    Think of it as an agreed design for locks and keys. Because everyone follows the same design, passkeys work across the major operating systems, browsers and password managers.

    Passkeys

    A passkey replaces your password. It's a secret kept on your device, and you unlock it the same way you unlock your device: with your fingerprint, face or PIN.

    Your fingerprint or face is not sent to the website. It only unlocks the passkey on your device.

    SituationPasswordPasskey

    Two kinds of passkey

    Synced
    Copied to your other devices through an account such as iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager. If you lose your phone, your other devices still have it.
    Device-bound
    Stays on one device, such as a security key. It offers the strongest protection, but you need a backup plan in case you lose it.

    You can see which kind you made in the backedUp field on the Try it tab. true means it's synced.

    Digital wallets

    A digital wallet is an app that keeps on your phone what you'd keep in a real wallet. Apple Wallet and Google Wallet are well-known examples.

    Payment cards
    Tap your phone to pay.
    Tickets and passes
    Boarding passes, event tickets and loyalty cards.
    Digital ID
    Driving licences, ID cards and diplomas, where your country or issuer supports them. These are called digital credentials. A trusted issuer, such as a government, signs them so that others can check they're real.

    The useful part is that you approve what gets shared, and you can share only what's needed. For example, you could prove you're over 18 without giving your full identity.

    The EU requires its member countries to offer citizens an identity wallet by the end of 2026. FIDO began work on wallets in December 2025, and that work is still in progress.

    A crypto wallet is something else. It holds the keys for cryptocurrency.

    Passkeys and wallets together

    They answer different questions, so one doesn't replace the other.

    A passkey says: "It's me again."
    It proves you own the account, and it tells the site nothing else about you.
    A wallet ID says: "Here's a fact about me, and someone trusted confirms it."
    It proves something like your age or your driving licence.

    Example: renting a car online

    1. Sign in to the rental site with a passkey.
    2. Prove you can drive with the digital driving licence in your wallet.
    3. Pay with the card in your wallet.

    On most phones, your passkeys are kept in your password manager, not in the app called Wallet.
